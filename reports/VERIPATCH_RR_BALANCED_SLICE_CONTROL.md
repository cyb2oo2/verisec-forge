## Standing Rule 8 check: is `0.5000` really the strongest control here?

The sign control is at chance *by construction* on this slice, which invites the
objection that it is a weak baseline. Two checks:

**Train-fitted search.** A search over the semantics-free feature space
(`char_net`, `|char_net|`, both signs, 400 thresholds) fit on the 6,000-row
training split selects threshold `0.0` — the plain sign rule, train accuracy
`0.8217`. The legitimate strongest semantics-free control on this slice is
therefore genuinely `0.5000`.

**Adversarial upper bound.** A rule *refit on the balanced evaluation slice
itself* — not a legitimate baseline, an upper bound — reaches:

| Rule | canonical | equivariance | both-correct |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `char_net`, threshold `-131.5`, refit on eval | `0.5812` | `0.3084` | `0.2078` |
| baseline antisym | `0.5552` | exact | `0.5552` |

It buys canonical accuracy (`0.5812` > `0.5552`) by **destroying equivariance**
(`0.3084`), so its both-directions-correct collapses to `0.2078`. A shifted
threshold is not an antisymmetric rule, and no antisymmetric character-count rule
can beat `0.5` on a sign-balanced slice — antisymmetrising `char_net` reduces to
the sign rule, and `|char_net|` cancels entirely.

So the `+0.0552` margin on both-directions-correct survives both checks. On
*canonical accuracy alone* an eval-tuned rule still exceeds the models, which is
why the relational metric is the one that carries the claim.

## Interpretation

1. **A small content-based signal exists.** After the polarity shortcut is
   removed, the antisymmetric systems retain `0.5552` / `0.5422`
   both-directions-correct against a `0.5000` control, CIs excluding zero. This
   is the first evidence in this investigation that the models carry relational
   information beyond diff shape.
2. **It is small.** `0.5552` against a `0.5000` floor, on 308 pairs. It is a
   detectable effect, not a competent system.
3. **It is glyph-bound.** The same systems are at chance on the prose rendering
   (`+0.0130`, `p=0.79`). Whatever residual signal exists does not survive
   re-encoding, consistent with the glyph-channel binding finding.
4. **Only the projection extracts it.** Independent inference is significantly
   *worse* than the control on both-directions-correct in every condition. The
   effect appears only through the antisymmetric readout.
5. **Fine-tuning does not help.** The repaired checkpoint (`0.5422`) does not
   exceed the frozen baseline (`0.5552`) on the balanced slice.

## Claim boundary

308 balanced pairs (25% of the clean suite), one control family, two checkpoints,
one length (1024), one seed. The balanced slice trades statistical power for
shortcut neutralisation; a `+0.055` effect at n=308 should be replicated on an
independently balanced sample before being built upon. Residual magnitude
structure is not removed by sign balancing (see
`docs/PURIFIED_EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md` §4), though the adversarial check above
shows it cannot be exploited without sacrificing equivariance.
