## The residual rejections have a different cause than DeltaSecommits

CrossVul contains **zero** single-line `code` records, yet 153 pairs still fail
the invariant. Diagnosing them separates two independent defects:

| Cause | Affected | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newline-free source records | 100% of DeltaSecommits, 1.75% of PatchEval | ingestion (`normalize_code_for_diff`) |
| **`difflib` `autojunk` asymmetry** | ~3% of every source | invariant, or `autojunk=False` |

`difflib.SequenceMatcher` computes its "popular element" junk set from the
*second* sequence only, so `SequenceMatcher(a, b)` and `SequenceMatcher(b, a)`
can match differently once a sequence reaches the 200-element autojunk
threshold. Measured on the 153 CrossVul rejections:

- 132 / 153 (86.27%) involve a side with ≥ 200 lines.
- 128 / 153 (83.66%) become exact mirrors when recomputed with `autojunk=False`.

Example (`crossvul-2483_1`, 248 vs 210 lines): forward diff is
`add=3, rem=42`; the reverse is `add=41, rem=4`. A true mirror would be
`add=42, rem=3`.

**This corrects an over-broad statement in the earlier report.** "`difflib` is
not the culprit" was correct for the DeltaSecommits failure (100%, caused by
newlines) but wrong as a general claim: `autojunk` asymmetry is a real, separate
cause of roughly 3% non-mirror pairs on every source. Both defects are now
caught — one fixed at ingestion, one rejected at construction.

Passing `autojunk=False` in the renderer would recover most of the rejected 3%
and is worth doing, but it requires a custom `unified_diff` since the stdlib
wrapper does not expose the flag. The invariant makes this an optimization
rather than a correctness issue.
